> This is the source material for the company **website** and **philosophy page**.

## 1. What the company does
A **global technology company** offering:
- Web development
- Web application development
- Mobile application development

And, in time, a **product**: a **GPS tracking system** sold to multiple clients.

Brand architecture (planned):
```
[Company / parent brand]
├─ Services: web · web-app · mobile-app development
└─ Product:  GPS tracking system  ← its own product name/sub-brand
```
> The company is an **umbrella brand** so it can hold both services *and* products, and grow without ever renaming.

## 2. Purpose (why we exist)
**We help businesses grow with technology.**
If a business is looking for growth, we're here to make it happen — with the right tech.

We're not a dev shop. We're a **growth partner who happens to wield tech.**

---

## 3. Vision (the world we're building toward)
**To enable businesses to do more in the world** — using technology to raise their
capacity, their involvement, and their **intensity**, so they experience and achieve
far more than they could alone.

---

## 4. The core philosophy (the soul of the company)

### In my own words (founder's narration — keep this voice)
> "Every second counts — use it to its fullest. Don't just *develop* the product, **live** it.
>
> Metaphorically: as humans, we experience the world around us only as much as we're
> *enabled* to. The more we enable ourselves, the more we can be involved in the world —
> and the more involved we are, the more **intense** our life becomes. We should all
> increase our intensity.
>
> By introducing tech to a business, I want to increase *their* intensity and the
> experiencing of their business — so their business can **do more in this world**."

### What it means
Technology is an **amplifier of life**, not just a tool. Just as a more capable human
experiences and does more, a more enabled business engages more, reaches further, and
lives more intensely. Our job is to raise that intensity.

---

## 5. Core principles (how we operate)
*Drawn directly from the founder's words.*

**1. Ship fast, learn faster.**
Build fast, deploy fast — so the client can test their business idea in the real world
and improve quickly, before betting big. *Speed is a growth tool, not a shortcut.*

**2. Best quality, shortest time.**
If a client wants quality, we're the choice. We compress the timeline without ever
compressing the craft. *No trade-off between fast and good.*

**3. The work is the standard.**
I give 100% to the task in hand — not to impress the client, but because it has to look
right *to my own eye*. *Integrity of craft over performance.*

**4. Total focus wins.**
Full attention on the point that matters most — because focusing on the right thing
produces the most favorable outcome. *Leverage over effort.*

**5. Enable, don't just build.**
We don't hand over a product — we raise what a business is *capable* of. More reach,
more involvement, more intensity. *Tech as an amplifier, not just a deliverable.*

## 8. Naming direction & constraints
- **Global standard.** The name must NOT be tied to a region or locality — this is a
  global company. A global ear should be able to say and trust it.
- **Umbrella / brandable**, not descriptive (must fit both services and a product).
- **Meaning encoded, not literal.** Sanskrit-rooted names (*Adikrishna, Dimith, Adimith*)
  read as regional/Indian — beautiful, but may conflict with "globally neutral."
  If the global rule is hard, lean toward **abstract / coined / Latin-root** names
  (cf. Vercel, Stripe, Twilio, Asana) that can still *encode* the meaning of focus,
  intensity, enablement, or the source.
- Candidate meaning-directions for a neutral name: **focus · intensity · amplify ·
  enable · elevate · ignite · the source / the first.**

### Name option pool (parked, from earlier)
- Personal blends: Adikrishna, Adikrish, Adimith, Dimith, Krishdin, Krismith, Adikrin
- Sound rule: 2–3 syllables max; one name carries the wordmark, others live in story/logo.
